# B472 - Source-Grounded Observations: Capture Who Did What, When, With Evidence

State: Phase 0 in progress (code audit done 2026-10-09; store audit queued as R20 on engramist/hippocampy#278)
Priority: P1

### Problem Statement

Campy's write path turns conversations into a graph built for agent and project
memory: decisions, constraints, plans, and typed relations between concepts
(REQUIRES, ENABLES, CHOSEN_OVER, REPLACES, ...). It has no place for the most
common kind of thing people say about themselves and others:

- an **event** with participants and a time: "Caroline went to a support group
  on 7 May", "I started running today";
- an **attribute** of a person or thing: "my sister is a nurse", "my dog is a
  golden retriever";
- a **changed state**: "I moved to Denver last week".

Those facts stay as raw `Message` text. Answering a question about them falls
back to text retrieval over turns, with no help from the graph.

Measured in campy-benchmarks on published results (hippocampy `71041ab` and
`367d35f`, llama3.1:8b, judge gemma4:26b):

- **The graph section never appears.** In all 60 LoCoMo-10 questions, the
  bundle has at most two sections: raw conversation turns, and a list of bare
  concept labels. `_stage_graph_structure` (Concept-to-Concept traversal)
  returned nothing for any of them. The same holds on LongMemEval and DMR.
- **The concept labels are mostly noise:**
  - LoCoMo-10 stores "Caroline", "Caroline!", "Congrats Caroline" and "Wow
    Caroline" as four separate Concepts. Surface variants are not merged.
  - DMR stores "Speaker" and "1, 7 days" as concepts. They come from the
    `[Session 1, 7 days 8 hours ago] Speaker 1:` prefix the harness writes
    into the turn text, because `notify_turn` has no field for the speaker or
    for when the turn happened.
  - Relative times ("ten years ago", "about five years ago", "this summer")
    become concepts with no anchor date. "Ten years ago" is useless without
    the turn's own date.
- **Scores by category show the cost.** LoCoMo-10 (60 questions, newest run):
  multi-hop accuracy 0.000 (evidence recall 0.153), temporal 0.417. These are
  the categories where a graph of people, events and times should beat plain
  text retrieval, and where Campy gets nothing from its graph today.
- **Retrieval at scale suffers too.** LongMemEval `s` (about 500 turns per
  question) has evidence recall 0.139, against 0.613 on the oracle variant.
  With no structured layer to narrow the search, every question is a text
  search over every turn.

This is not mainly a benchmark problem. The same gap shows up in agent memory:
"who changed the CI config, and when?", "what did we tell the client about the
deadline last month?", "which teammate owns the billing service?" are events,
attributes and times as well.

### Prior art this card follows

The design follows a nine-step, source-grounded extraction pipeline the user
built for another project, which turns meeting transcripts into a governed
graph. That pipeline proved, by measurement on its own corpus:

- **Grounded or absent.** Every extracted assertion anchors to a verbatim source
  span (speaker, time, turn). An assertion that cannot cite its source is not
  stored.
- **Entity resolution is a real gate, not a nicety.** Independent extraction
  runs minted three identifiers for one real-world project. Resolving
  identities before loading is what keeps the graph from fragmenting, the same
  failure as Campy's four "Caroline" concepts.
- **Discovery proposes; it does not decide.** Lexical, structural and
  model-assisted routes propose candidates. The final class comes later, from
  rules applied to accepted evidence.
- **Bounded model questions.** When evidence is unresolved, the model is asked a
  narrow question whose answer is validated against the exact source turns.
  The answer can add evidence. It cannot mint terms or decide a class on its
  own.
- **Conservative promotion.** A weaker construct is the default when evidence is
  thin. In that corpus, only four settled decisions were minted from six
  meetings, which was correct.
- **Traceable, recomputable records.** Every conclusion can be explained back to
  its evidence, and unresolved cases are visible rather than hidden.

Campy is not that pipeline:
- it ingests every turn of every session, live;
- it runs locally on an 8B model;
- no human reviews each step.

So this card adopts the principles and adapts each step. One step is dropped and
one is made optional. All nine are listed below so none gets lost.

### The nine steps, adapted to Campy

Each step lists what Campy has today, the gap, and the change. **Phase** says
which part of this card builds it (see Implementation Plan below).

#### Step 1 - Preserve source identity (Phase 1)

*Principle:* keep the source, the turn, the speaker and the time as structured
data, not as text inside the turn.

*Today:*
- `Message` nodes hold role, session, text and `created_at`.
- B312 provenance (`source`, `observed_at`, `evidence_ref`) exists on
  fact-bearing tables. `Message` is excluded, since it is bookkeeping.
- `notify_turn` accepts `role`, `content`, `session_id`, git context, and the
  `external_app_id` / `external_session_id` labels.

*Gap:*
- There is no `speaker` and no `occurred_at` on a turn.
- A caller that knows them has to write them into the text, and Steps 1-2 then
  extract the prefix as concepts ("Speaker", "1, 7 days", dates).
- `created_at` is ingest time. For backfill, imports or replayed history it is
  not when the turn happened, so relative dates resolve against the wrong day.

*Change:*
- `notify_turn` gains optional `speaker` (free text: a name, or "user" /
  "assistant") and `occurred_at` (ISO timestamp). Both are stored on `Message`.
- When absent, `speaker` defaults from `role` and `occurred_at` from
  `created_at`, so existing callers are unchanged.
- Concept extraction (Steps 1-2 of the Gated Consolidation Loop) runs on the
  content only, never on metadata.
- A turn's "time" for every later step is `occurred_at`.

#### Step 2 - Govern the vocabulary: concepts, variants, conditions (Phase 2)

*Principle:* maintain one reviewed set of concepts with their variants. The
vocabulary describes things; it does not decide facts.

*Today:*
- SKOS label accumulation already exists: alternate labels wired to a concept,
  each with its own embedding, and pruned when unused.
- The domain dictionary (B160) and the disambiguation queue (B158) exist.

*Gap:* on conversational data, surface variants become separate Concepts
("Caroline", "Caroline!", "Congrats Caroline", "Wow Caroline") instead of
alternate labels on one entity. Greetings and interjections around a name are
not stripped.

*Change:*
- Normalize candidate surface forms before minting. Strip punctuation and
  vocative or greeting wrappers ("Congrats X", "Wow X", "X!").
- Match against existing canonical labels and alternate labels: exact first,
  then embedding.
- Attach a variant as an alternate label instead of minting a new Concept.
- Gray-zone pairs go to the existing B158 disambiguation queue. They are
  usable meanwhile as separate tentative entities.
- Speaker names from Step 1 seed the canonical person list for a session, the
  same role the meeting pipeline's known-people list plays.

#### Step 3 - Source-grounded observations (Phase 3, the core of this card)

*Principle:* store what was said as observations, each with:
- its source span;
- its speaker;
- its extraction method;
- its uncertainty.

They are kept apart from conclusions drawn from them.

*Today:*
- Fact-bearing nodes exist only for the decision, constraint and plan family.
- The schema.org routing table (Step 3 of the Loop) already names the shapes
  needed: `gist:Event -> schema:Event (startDate, endDate, location,
  organizer, ...)`, `gist:Agent -> schema:Person (name, jobTitle, ...)`,
  `gist:Magnitude -> schema:QuantitativeValue`.
- Those properties are passed to the model as context, but nothing persists
  them as structured fields.

*Change:* a new fact-bearing node, `Observation`, in `PROVENANCE_TABLES`:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `subject` | Canonical entity id (Step 2), e.g. the person |
| `predicate` | A small controlled set first: `did` (event), `has_attribute`, `is_a`, `located_in`, `owns`, `relates_to`, `prefers`, `changed_to`; extend by review, not by the model |
| `object` | Entity id or literal value |
| `event_text` | Short normalized description for events ("attended an LGBTQ support group") |
| `time_start` / `time_end` | Absolute, resolved by Step 8; nullable |
| `time_text` | The original time phrase ("last Saturday") |
| `time_precision` | `day` / `week` / `month` / `year` / `unknown` |
| `speaker` | Who asserted it (from Step 1). Not always the subject |
| `polarity` | `asserted` / `negated` / `hypothetical` / `planned` |
| `confidence` | 0-1. The existing tiers apply: < 0.60 not stored, 0.60-0.90 `confidence_low`, > 0.90 full |
| `extraction_method` | `deterministic` / `pattern` / `llm` |
| `evidence_ref` | The source `Message` id (required) |
| `evidence_span` | Character offsets of the verbatim supporting text in that message (required) |
| `observed_at` | The source turn's `occurred_at` |
| provenance + supersession columns | As B312 (`source`, `source_version`, `superseded_by`, ...) |

Edges:
- `(Observation)-[ABOUT]->(Concept)` for the subject and any entity object.
- `(Observation)-[EVIDENCED_BY]->(Message)`.
- `DEPRECATED_BY` (B312) between observations when one replaces another
  (Step 8).

**Grounded or absent:**
- An observation without a valid `evidence_span` is rejected at write time,
  enforced in the write API, not left to convention.
- The span must be a substring of the cited message's stored text, after
  whitespace normalization.

#### Step 4 - Discover candidates through several routes (Phase 3)

*Principle:* discovery proposes candidates; it does not assign the final class.

*Today:* the Step 4 "senses" decide whether anything structured is stored.
Their triggers are tuned to project language (decided, must, plan to, ...), so
"I adopted a puppy" fires none of them and becomes noise.

*Change:* add candidate routes that propose observations whether or not a
decision sense fires:
- *Structural:* spaCy subject-verb-object triples with a PERSON or known-entity
  subject (Step 1b already parses these; today only the verb whitelist turns
  them into edges).
- *Lexical:* first-person attribute and event patterns ("I am / I work as /
  my X is / I went to / I started / I moved to"), each with its span.
- *Temporal:* any DATE or TIME entity, or relative time phrase, attached to a
  candidate in the same clause.
- *Model-assisted:* only through Step 7, never free-form.

Candidates are proposals with a confidence. The existing senses keep deciding
everything they decide today. Nothing in the decision family changes.

#### Step 5 - Resolve links by evidence, not proximity (Phase 4)

*Principle:* connect observations to the right subject, to each other and to
earlier observations using evidence, not mere co-occurrence.

*Today:* the only relation that reliably forms on conversational data is
`CO_OCCURS_WITH`, which is proximity. The typed relations need verb or decision
language.

*Change:*
- Resolve each observation's subject and object to canonical entities (Step 2).
  Pronouns and "I" / "my" resolve to the turn's speaker (Step 1). Third-person
  pronouns resolve only when the clause or the previous turn names exactly one
  candidate. Otherwise the observation stays unresolved, and is visible as
  such.
- Link an observation to the earlier observation it updates (same subject and
  predicate, a newer time, a different object) as a supersession candidate for
  Step 8.
- Link event observations that share a subject and an overlapping time window,
  for multi-hop questions ("what did Caroline do the week after the support
  group?").
- Co-occurrence stays as a weak signal. It never creates an observation link by
  itself.

#### Step 6 - Responsibility and authority (deferred; recorded so it is not forgotten)

*Principle in the source pipeline:* derive who has responsibility, membership,
remit and decision authority, under a written policy, and keep those distinct.

*Campy:* nothing comparable today, and nothing needed for personal or
conversational memory. It does become relevant to agent memory in teams: who
may approve a change, who owns a service, whether a statement came from someone
with authority to make the decision. Campy already distinguishes
`source`/`authority` (B312/B317).

*Status:* **out of scope for this card.** Revisit when multi-user or team
workspaces (B315/B316) need "who decided" with authority. The `speaker` field
(Step 1) and `subject` (Step 3) are the inputs it would need.

#### Step 7 - Bounded model questions, validated against the source (Phase 3)

*Principle:* when evidence is unresolved, ask the model a narrow question, then
validate the answer against the request, the source turns and the citations. An
answer may add evidence. It may not create terms or decide the final class.

*Today:* Steps 2, 3b and 6 of the Loop call the local LLM, now off the event
loop (B468), with forced JSON output. Answers are not checked against the
source text.

*Change:*
- One bounded extraction call per batch of turns, in the consolidation sweep,
  not per turn:
  - the input is the turns with their ids;
  - the output is a JSON list of candidate observations, each with `message_id`
    and a verbatim `quote`.
- Validation:
  - `quote` must be a substring of that message (whitespace-normalized);
  - `predicate` must be in the controlled set;
  - subjects must resolve (Step 5) or the observation is stored as unresolved;
  - anything failing validation is dropped and counted.
- The model may not:
  - add predicates;
  - merge entities, which is Step 2's job;
  - set a supersession, which is Step 8's job.
- A drop-rate metric (rejected / proposed) is logged to `activity.log`, so a
  model that paraphrases instead of quoting is visible.
- Config: `[observations] llm_batch_turns` (default 20) and `llm_enabled`
  (default true). With the LLM disabled, Steps 4 and 8 still produce
  deterministic and pattern observations.

#### Step 8 - Deterministic rules on accepted evidence (Phase 4)

*Principle:* versioned, deterministic rules turn accepted evidence into
conclusions. Unknown or candidate evidence cannot silently become accepted.

*Change:* rules, versioned in code with the rule version recorded on what they
write:
- **Temporal normalization.**
  - Resolve `time_text` against the source turn's `occurred_at` (Step 1):
    "yesterday", "last Saturday", "two weeks ago", "ten years ago", "this
    summer", "in May".
  - Set `time_start` / `time_end` / `time_precision`. Unparseable phrases keep
    `time_precision = unknown` and are never guessed.
  - This is the single biggest lever on temporal questions, and it needs no
    model.
- **Supersession.** A newer observation with the same subject and a
  single-valued predicate (`located_in`, `has_attribute:job`, ...) and a
  different object supersedes the older one via B312 `DEPRECATED_BY`. Which
  predicates are single-valued is part of the rule set, not a model judgment.
  This reuses B463/B464's supersession path.
- **Promotion.**
  - Observations stay `confidence_low` until a second independent observation
    or a deterministic route confirms them.
  - A single LLM-route observation is usable in retrieval but marked tentative.
- **Candidate never becomes accepted silently.** Every promotion records the
  rule and the evidence ids.

#### Step 9 - Traceable records; review and recompute (Phase 5; review optional)

*Principle:* make conclusions searchable and explainable, expose unresolved
cases, review proposed changes, then recompute affected conclusions. Correction
and retraction need separate proof.

*Today:* `campy review` (memory audit), `flagged_for_review`, and the B158
queue.

*Change:*
- **Retrieval.** A new bundle stage, `_stage_observations`. Section type
  `observations`, placed after exact facts and before conversation. It:
  - ranks observations by subject match (entities in the query, resolved via
    Step 2 labels), then predicate and time fit, then embedding similarity;
  - renders each one with its time and source, e.g.
    `[observed 2023-05-07, from Caroline] Caroline attended an LGBTQ support group`;
  - lets the answering model check the cited turn when needed.
- **Recall citations.** Each observation item carries its `evidence_ref`, so the
  harness's evidence recall can count an observation as finding its source
  turn.
- **Unresolved cases.** `campy review` lists unresolved subjects and
  low-confidence observations.
- **Recompute.** When an entity merge happens (B158 resolve) or the rule version
  changes, a sweep task re-resolves and re-normalizes the affected
  observations.
- **Human review stays optional.** Campy cannot wait on a reviewer. Unreviewed
  observations are usable at their confidence. Retraction (an observation
  proven wrong) goes through the existing supersession and flagged paths, with
  its own evidence.

### Implementation Plan

Each phase is shippable alone and measured before the next starts.

**Phase 0 - Audit (no behaviour change).**
1. On a kept LoCoMo-10 store and a kept LongMemEval `s` store, count node types
   and edge types.
2. List the 50 most frequent Concept labels.
3. Confirm whether any `schema:Event` / `schema:Person` properties from Step 3
   routing are persisted anywhere today.
4. Write the counts into this card. They are the "before" picture for Phases
   1-2.

**Phase 0 results.**

*Code audit (2026-10-09, main `402c96c`), confirming the measurements above:*

1. **Concept text is raw spaCy output.**
   - `step1_ner.extract_entities` takes every `doc.ents` span, and noun chunks
     when NER finds 1 or fewer, on the full turn content, including any
     prefix a caller wrote into it.
   - DATE, TIME and CARDINAL entities pass unless `_is_junk_entity` drops
     them. It drops bare cardinals ("five"), not phrases ("ten years ago",
     "1, 7 days"). So relative times become Concepts with no anchor, and a
     harness prefix like `[Session 1, 7 days 8 hours ago] Speaker 1:` becomes
     concepts.
2. **Dedup is exact text, case-insensitive (B33,
   `orchestrator.find_concept_by_exact_text`).**
   - No punctuation or wrapper normalization, no embedding match. So
     "Caroline", "Caroline!" and "Congrats Caroline" (a span spaCy returns
     as one entity) are three Concepts.
   - Alternate labels are not attached at concept creation.
3. **No schema.org property values are stored anywhere.**
   - `route_to_schema_org` returns a type and its property *names*.
   - The names only reach the Step 3b prompt as context.
   - `orchestrator.create_concept` persists `gist_class` and the
     `schema_org_type` string, nothing else.
   - The literal properties on a Concept are: `anomaly_type`, `archived`,
     `concept_id`, `confidence`, `confidence_low`, `created_at`,
     `embedding_dim`, `embedding_model`, `flagged_for_review`, `gist_class`,
     `last_accessed_at`, `pathway_strength`, `salience_score`,
     `schema_org_type`, `text_raw`.
   - Checked with `diag_graph_audit.py` on a store written through these
     queries. An `Event` Concept has no `startDate` and no `location`.
     **This answers Phase 0 question 3: no.**
4. **Step 4 (`classify_artifact`) decides storage per entity using the
   decision-family senses.** Plain statements about people survive only as
   Concepts (via the noise floor) or not at all. Nothing records what was
   said about them.

*Store audit:* `campy-benchmarks/diag_graph_audit.py` (campy-benchmarks#29)
runs on the kept stores from R20 (LoCoMo-10, DMR, LongMemEval `s`). It
reports:
- node and edge counts by type;
- concepts by gist and schema.org type;
- the Concept property list;
- variant-split concepts, prefix junk and time-phrase concepts;
- the top 50 labels by pathway strength.

### Risks and open questions

1. **Cost.** A bounded LLM call per 20 turns on an 8B model, for a 500-turn
   history, is about 25 calls per LongMemEval question. That's acceptable in
   the sweep, but measure it. Deterministic and pattern routes must carry most
   of the load.
2. **Precision of a small model.** Grounded quotes stop invention, not
   misreading. The Phase 3 precision gate (>= 0.85) is there so Phase 4 does
   not build rules on bad observations.
3. **Overfitting to benchmarks.** The predicate set and patterns must come from
   general first-person and third-person statements, not from LoCoMo's
   questions. The agent-memory examples in the Problem Statement are part of
   the fixture set for that reason.
4. **Privacy.** Observations about third parties ("my sister is a nurse") are
   more explicit than raw turns. They follow the same workspace scoping and
   deletion paths as `Message`. Confirm `campy forget`-style deletion
   cascades to observations via `EVIDENCED_BY`.
5. **Open.** Should `Observation` reuse `FactEntity`/`FACT_*` (B317) instead of
   a new table? B317 is for externally owned facts (`authority = projected`).
   Observations are Campy-extracted claims, so the proposal is a separate
   table. Decide in review.
6. **Open.** Should `speaker` become a `Person` concept automatically? The
   proposal is yes for named speakers, and no for the generic "user" /
   "assistant".
