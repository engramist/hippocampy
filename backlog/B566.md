# B566: a fact whose subject is "I" or "we" is anchored on the speaker

**Status:** Open. Filed 2026-11-13 with B565. Not fixed.

**Principles:** P8 (subjects are the backbone), P3 (a rule decides).

**Why.** Before B565, facts whose subject text is "we", "it" or "I" could be anchored (`HAS_SUBJECT`) on a Concept named
"We", "It" or "I", which the Loop had created. B565 stops creating those Concepts and archives the existing ones.

On the five principled step-0c stores, **59 facts** are left without a live subject after the cleanup (conv-26 14,
conv-41 35, conv-48 4, conv-49 6, conv-50 0). A re-capture leaves the same facts unanchored, because the subject lookup
finds no Concept. Unanchored is honest (P8), but the speaker is usually knowable.

**Design sketch (for review before code).**
- **"I", "me", "my":** the turn's speaker. `observations/extract.py` already maps a first-person subject to the speaker
  (`display_subject, subject_id = speaker, speaker_id`). Find out why these facts did not take that path: the subject
  text may be "I" in a quote attributed to someone else, or a capitalised "I" missed by the first-person check. Fix the
  check rather than add a second path.
- **"we", "us", "our":** the speaker as `HAS_SUBJECT`, and the other people the turn names as `HAS_PARTICIPANT` (B564's
  partner role). The rule needs no model: the speaker comes from `SPOKEN_BY`, and the partners are the turn's admitted
  PERSON Concepts, when the fact's quote names them.
- **"it", "they", "something":** no rule. These stay unanchored and flagged, with no guess at the antecedent.
- **Measure:** step Q's "gold fact without the subject edge" class (90 on the cleaned copies) and query hits, on the
  same copies.
