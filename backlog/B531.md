# B531: trial dateparser against our own time parser

**Status:** Compared (B538); timeparse kept, dateparser not adopted

`campy/brain/temporal_lobe/observations/timeparse.py` (about 380 lines) normalises time phrases by hand (B484). dateparser (BSD-3, pure Python, maintained) handles relative dates ("next week", "last Friday") against a reference date.

**Work:** on every time phrase in a store copy, compare the dates timeparse and dateparser produce from each turn's own date. Report the disagreements. If dateparser is right more often, swap it in behind a flag and gate on LongMemEval temporal plus the LoCoMo holdout. Version or phase references stay vocabulary.

## Result (measured in B538)

Run on the user turns of five LoCoMo stores (3,042 turns): the spans timeparse finds were also resolved by dateparser from each turn's own date, and both were scored against the gold of the category-2 questions of R98 and R100 (64 parseable golds; best annotation on any evidence turn).

| | exact | overlapping | wrong | none |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| timeparse | 35 | 5 | 3 | 21 |
| dateparser, same spans | 13 | 22 | 2 | 27 |
| dateparser `search_dates` | 13 | 26 | 17 | 8 |

- dateparser returns one point. A week, month or year phrase ("last week", "next month") becomes a day inside the range, which is why most of its matches are "overlapping" and not "exact".
- It has no result for 53 of the 207 spans (for example `last Friday`), and `search_dates` adds spans that are not time expressions (17 wrong).
- Where both resolved, they never disagreed: 60 spans on the same day, 94 dateparser points inside timeparse's range.

So dateparser is not better, and it adds a dependency that pulls timezone and locale data. Not swapped in. To repeat: call `find_time_expressions` and `dateparser.parse(span, settings={'RELATIVE_BASE': ...})` on the same turn.
